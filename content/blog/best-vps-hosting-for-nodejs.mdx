## Introduction

A VPS gives your Node.js app a real server for a few dollars a month, with no cold starts, no per-invocation billing, and full control. Here are the providers worth running production on.

These are the options I rate, what sets them apart, and where each one earns its place.

The appeal of a VPS for Node is that it matches how Node actually likes to run. A Node process is happiest staying warm, holding a connection pool open to your database, keeping a WebSocket server alive, and running the occasional background job on an interval. Serverless platforms fight that model: they tear your process down, re-establish connections, and bill you per invocation. A VPS just keeps running. You get a predictable monthly price, root access, and the freedom to install whatever you need, in exchange for owning the parts a managed platform would have handled: the OS, the process manager, the reverse proxy, and the backups.

## How we compared

Benchmarks on an empty box tell you very little. We weighed these providers on how they behave once a real Node app is living on them:

- **Price-to-performance:** actual vCPU and RAM you get per dollar, not the headline.
- **Network.** Bandwidth allowance, egress cost, and region coverage near your users.
- **Day-two experience:** snapshots, backups, firewalls, and the quality of the dashboard.
- **Room to grow.** Can you resize in place, or add managed databases alongside?

## What to look for

Before comparing names, get clear on your own priorities. The factors that matter most here:

- **Fit for your stack.** The best tool is the one that matches how you already work.
- **Pricing and value.** Weigh the real cost against the time it saves you.
- **Developer experience:** good docs and a clean API compound every single day.
- **Scalability.** Will it still fit when your project (or team) grows?

For a Node app specifically, add one more criterion: how close the VPS sits to your database. A warm Node process talking to a database across a datacentre boundary will spend more time waiting on the network than running your code. Colocate the two in the same region, ideally the same provider, and your tail latencies improve without touching a line of application code.

## The top picks

### Hetzner Cloud

Absurd price-to-performance in Europe/US. The enthusiast favourite for self-hosted Node.

Hetzner is the clear value champion. For the price of a mid-tier droplet elsewhere you get noticeably more vCPU, RAM, and generous bandwidth, often on modern AMD or Arm hardware that benchmarks extremely well under Node's single-threaded-per-core workload. The trade-off historically was geography, since Hetzner's roots are European, though its US datacentres (Ashburn and Hillsboro) have closed much of that gap. The dashboard is clean, snapshots and firewalls are built in, and the API is pleasant to automate against. If you are price-sensitive, comfortable on the command line, and your users are in Europe or North America, Hetzner is tough to beat.

### DigitalOcean

Best docs and dashboard, one-click Node droplets, managed databases alongside.

DigitalOcean is the one to pick when you want the VPS experience without the rough edges. The one-click Node.js droplet gets you a configured box in minutes, the dashboard is the most approachable in the category, and the documentation is a real asset, since almost any problem you hit has a clear, correct tutorial a search away. The real advantage for a Node app is the surrounding ecosystem: managed Postgres, Redis, and Spaces object storage sit one click away and colocate with your droplet, so you can offload the stateful, scary parts while keeping your app on cheap compute. You pay a modest premium over Hetzner for this polish, and for most teams it is money well spent.

### Linode (Akamai)

Reliable, developer-friendly, strong global network.

Now part of Akamai, Linode pairs a long track record of reliability with access to one of the largest edge and CDN networks on the planet. The developer experience is solid and unpretentious, pricing is competitive, and the global region coverage is excellent, which helps if your users are spread across continents. It occupies a comfortable middle ground: not the absolute cheapest, not the flashiest, but dependable and well-supported, with the Akamai network behind it as a meaningful differentiator for latency-sensitive apps.

### Vultr

Widest region coverage and high-frequency compute options.

Vultr's standout features are geographic reach and its High Frequency and bare-metal compute tiers. If you need a server in a specific far-flung city, whether in South America, the Middle East, or parts of Asia-Pacific that the bigger players serve thinly, Vultr very often has one. The High Frequency instances run on faster clocked CPUs and NVMe storage, which suits latency-sensitive Node APIs where per-request speed matters more than raw core count. The dashboard and API are capable, and pricing is in the same competitive band as DigitalOcean and Linode.

### AWS Lightsail

AWS in simplicity mode: a flat-price VPS with an easy path to the wider AWS ecosystem.

Lightsail is AWS pretending to be a simple VPS host, and it does the impression well. You get flat monthly pricing, a friendly console, and bundled bandwidth, with none of the usual AWS billing anxiety. The strategic advantage is the escape hatch: when you outgrow Lightsail you can peer it into a full VPC and start adopting RDS, S3, or Lambda without migrating providers. If you suspect you will eventually want AWS's depth but do not want to face the full console today, Lightsail is the pragmatic on-ramp.

## Deploying Node on a VPS: the baseline

Whatever provider you pick, a production-ready Node box follows the same recipe. Run your app under a process manager so it restarts on crash and survives reboots, put a reverse proxy in front for TLS termination, and never expose Node directly on port 80/443.

```bash
# Keep the Node process alive and start on boot
npm install -g pm2
pm2 start dist/main.js --name api
pm2 startup
pm2 save

# Nginx reverse-proxies :443 -> Node on :3000, handling TLS
sudo apt install nginx certbot python3-certbot-nginx
sudo certbot --nginx -d api.example.com
```

That handful of steps is the entire "ops tax" for a small Node service, and it is a one-time cost. Automate it in a provisioning script once and every future box is minutes away.

## Common mistakes

The mistake that bites hardest is skipping backups until the day you need one. Enable automated snapshots on the provider and dump your database somewhere off-box. Running Node as root behind no firewall is the security equivalent, and it is avoidable in two minutes with a basic firewall rule set. On the performance side, teams routinely over-provision RAM and under-provision CPU; Node is CPU-bound per core, so for compute-heavy APIs more vCPU usually helps more than more memory. Finally, do not forget log rotation, because an unattended app will happily fill the disk with logs and take itself down.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which should I choose if I'm just starting out?**
Start with the option that has the gentlest learning curve and best documentation. You can always switch once you understand your needs better.

**Is the most popular option always the best?**
No. Popularity means more tutorials and community help, but the *right* choice depends on your specific stack, budget, and goals.

**Do I need Docker on a VPS?**
Not necessarily. A single Node service runs perfectly well under PM2 and Nginx. Docker earns its place once you have multiple services, want reproducible environments, or plan to move to orchestration later, but it is not a prerequisite for a production app.

**How much RAM does a Node app need?**
More than you'd guess, because of the V8 heap and your dependencies. 1GB is fine for a modest API; give yourself 2GB once you add a build step, caching, or image processing on the same box.

## Final verdict

Hetzner wins on raw value; DigitalOcean wins on developer experience. Either will happily run a production Node.js API.

Choose Hetzner if you are price-sensitive and your users sit in Europe or North America. Choose DigitalOcean if you want the smoothest ride and managed databases a click away. Pick Linode or Vultr when global reach is the deciding factor, and reach for Lightsail when you know AWS is in your future. Any of the five will keep a Node process warm and happy. The differences come down to your wallet, your users' geography, and how much polish you want around the edges.
