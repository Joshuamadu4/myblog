## Introduction

Postman is powerful, but its account requirements and heft push many developers to look elsewhere. Somewhere between the mandatory sign-in, the creeping memory footprint, and collections that live in a cloud you don't control, the tool that once felt convenient starts to feel like a tax. The good news: the ecosystem around it has exploded, and several alternatives keep the parts you actually use while dropping the rest.

Here are the strongest options, with a clear recommendation at the end. I've migrated teams off Postman more than once, so the notes below focus on the practical realities of switching: how collections port over, where the friction hides, and which tool actually sticks.

## What to look for

Before comparing names, get clear on your own priorities. The factors that matter most here:

- **Fit for your stack:** the best tool is the one that matches how you already work. If your team reviews everything in pull requests, a file-based client wins instantly.
- **Pricing and value.** Weigh the real cost against the time it saves you. Much of what drives people off Postman is per-seat pricing and paywalled sync.
- **Developer experience.** Good docs and a clean API compound every single day. Fast startup, no forced login, and sane keyboard navigation are the little things that matter.
- **Scalability.** Will it still fit when your project (or team) grows? Consider how collections are shared and whether that model survives a bigger team.
- **Import path.** Can it ingest your existing Postman or OpenAPI collections? A tool you can't migrate *to* is a tool you won't adopt.

### How we compared

Each alternative was judged on four things: how it stores and shares state (cloud vs. local files), startup speed and resource use, protocol coverage (REST, GraphQL, gRPC, WebSocket), and how painlessly it imports an existing Postman collection. The winners remove the friction Postman adds rather than piling on the most features.

## The top picks

### Bruno

Stores collections as plain files in your repo, perfect for version control and reviews. This is the single biggest reason teams switch. Bruno's `.bru` text format sits beside your code, so API changes show up in diffs, go through code review, and never depend on an external account or sync service. There's no mandatory login and no telemetry gate. For a team that treats infrastructure as code, Bruno treats your API collection the same way.

It's younger than Postman, so a handful of advanced collaboration and mocking features aren't as deep, and you're committing to its file conventions. But it imports Postman collections, so trying it costs almost nothing. **Choose Bruno when** you want your API requests committed and reviewed like source code.

### Insomnia

Fast, clean, and open-core with strong GraphQL and gRPC support. Insomnia has long been the obvious "I just want a nicer Postman" pick. The UI is tidy, environment management is straightforward, and the GraphQL explorer is very good. The open-source core keeps the basics free; paid tiers add team sync.

The caveat is that ownership and account-requirement changes over the years have made some users wary, so weigh long-term stability. **Choose Insomnia when** you want a polished desktop client with excellent GraphQL/gRPC and minimal fuss.

### Hoppscotch

Free, open-source, and runs in the browser with a tiny footprint. There's nothing to install and nothing to log into for basic use: open the tab, send a request. It covers REST, GraphQL, and realtime protocols (WebSocket, SSE, MQTT), and you can self-host an internal instance behind your VPN. For quick checks against staging, it's the fastest path from "I need to test this" to a response.

Being browser-based, CORS restrictions can interfere when hitting services that don't allow it, and very large workflows feel cramped. **Choose Hoppscotch when** you want an instant, zero-install client or a self-hostable internal tool.

### Yaak

A newer native client focused on speed and a Git-friendly local workflow. Yaak is built to be fast and local-first: it stores data on disk, supports environment variables and chained requests, and leans into the "no cloud account required" philosophy. It's a strong middle ground between Bruno's file-in-repo purity and Insomnia's polish.

As the newest entry, its ecosystem and plugin story are still maturing, so verify it covers any niche protocol you depend on. **Choose Yaak when** you want a snappy native app with a local-first, Git-friendly bent and a modern UI.

### HTTPie / curl

Terminal-first tools that pair perfectly with scripts and CI. `curl` is everywhere and copy-pasteable; `HTTPie` wraps the same power in a far friendlier syntax with colourised, formatted output. Neither is a collection manager, but for one-off requests, debugging, and reproducible bug reports, they're unbeatable, and they're already installed or one package away.

```bash
# HTTPie: readable and expressive
http POST api.example.com/login email=me@example.com password=secret

# curl: universal and scriptable
curl -s -X POST api.example.com/login \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"email":"me@example.com","password":"secret"}'
```

**Choose HTTPie/curl when** you want to script requests, embed them in CI, or hand someone a reproducible command.

## Who this is for

Git-centric teams should look at Bruno or Yaak first; committing collections changes how an entire team reasons about API changes. Developers who want a straight upgrade in polish without rethinking their workflow will be happiest on Insomnia. Anyone who just needs a fast throwaway check should keep Hoppscotch bookmarked. And every developer should be fluent in curl regardless of which GUI they adopt.

## Migration tips

- **Export before you switch.** Postman exports collections as JSON; Bruno, Insomnia, and Yaak can all import that format. Do a dry run with one collection before moving everything.
- **Re-create environments, don't trust the import.** Environment variables and secrets often don't carry over cleanly. Rebuild them deliberately and keep real secrets out of anything committed.
- **Move team-shared collections last.** Migrate your personal workflow first, prove the tool, then bring the team along once you can answer their questions.
- **Keep Postman around briefly.** Run both in parallel for a sprint so nobody is blocked if an edge case surfaces.

## Pricing at a glance

Bruno, Hoppscotch, HTTPie, and curl are free and open source with no per-seat cost. Yaak is free and local-first. Insomnia's core is free with paid sync and collaboration tiers. For teams specifically trying to escape Postman's per-seat billing, almost every option here removes that line item entirely.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which should I choose if I'm just starting out?**
Start with the option that has the gentlest learning curve and best documentation. Hoppscotch or Insomnia get you productive fastest. You can always switch once you understand your needs better.

**Is the most popular option always the best?**
No. Popularity means more tutorials and community help, but the *right* choice depends on your specific stack, budget, and goals. For many teams the less-famous Bruno is the better fit precisely because it's Git-native.

**Can I import my existing Postman collections?**
Yes. Bruno, Insomnia, and Yaak all import Postman's JSON export. Expect to rebuild environments and secrets by hand, but the requests themselves usually come across cleanly.

**Do these support GraphQL and gRPC?**
Insomnia has the strongest GraphQL and gRPC story; Hoppscotch covers GraphQL and realtime protocols well; Bruno and Yaak handle REST and GraphQL comfortably. Match the tool to the protocols you actually use.

## Final verdict

For most developers Bruno is the standout Postman replacement thanks to file-based collections you can commit alongside your code. If you want polish over Git-purity, pick Insomnia; for instant checks, Hoppscotch; for a modern local-first native app, Yaak; and keep HTTPie or curl in your pocket for everything scriptable.
