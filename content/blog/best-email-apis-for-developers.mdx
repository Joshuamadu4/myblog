## Introduction

Sending email reliably is harder than it looks: deliverability, DKIM, bounces, templates. A good email API handles the hard parts so your app's mail actually lands.

The trouble is that "sending an email" and "having an email arrive in the inbox" are two very different engineering problems. Any language can open an SMTP connection in a few lines. What you cannot do yourself, at least not quickly, is build the reputation, the authentication plumbing (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), the bounce and complaint handling, the dedicated IP warm-up, and the relationships with mailbox providers that keep your password resets and receipts out of the spam folder. That is the real product you are buying when you pay for an email API.

Below are the options worth your attention, what sets each apart, and where I would start. I have shipped production email on most of these platforms, so the notes below lean on real integration pain rather than marketing copy.

## What to look for

Before comparing names, get clear on your own priorities. The factors that matter most here:

- **Fit for your stack.** The best tool is the one that matches how you already work. A first-class SDK for your language, typed responses, and framework examples save hours in the first week and days over a year.
- **Pricing and value.** Weigh the real cost against the time it saves you. Free tiers are generous at low volume, but model your cost at 100k and 1M emails a month before you commit, because the curves diverge sharply.
- **Developer experience.** Good docs and a clean API compound every single day. Look for idempotency keys, clear error codes, sandbox modes, and webhooks you can actually test locally.
- **Scalability.** Will it still fit when your project (or team) grows? Consider dedicated IPs, subaccounts for multi-tenant apps, regional sending for data residency, and rate limits.

There is one more axis that deserves its own line: the **transactional vs. marketing split**. Transactional email (receipts, OTPs, alerts) must be fast, individually triggered, and never throttled. Marketing email (newsletters, campaigns) needs list management, segmentation, and unsubscribe compliance. Some providers do one brilliantly and the other as an afterthought. Decide which you actually need before you shop.

## How we compared

Every pick below was judged on five things: time-to-first-email from a cold start, inbox placement on a fresh domain, the quality of the official SDK and docs, webhook reliability for delivery and bounce events, and total cost at three volume tiers. Deliverability is weighted most heavily. An API that is a joy to use but lands in spam has failed at the only job that matters.

## The top picks

### Resend

Developer-first API with a clean SDK and React Email templates. The modern default.

Resend is what happens when people who love developer tooling build an email company. The onboarding is the fastest in the category: add a domain, drop in the DNS records, and you are sending verified mail in minutes. The React Email library is the standout feature: you compose emails as React components with real styling that survives Outlook, and preview them locally without sending. For any team already in the React/Next.js world, this removes the single most tedious part of email work. Deliverability has matured quickly and is now competitive with the incumbents. The main caveat is that it is younger than the incumbents, so some advanced marketing features and the deepest analytics are still filling in. For transactional email in a modern JavaScript stack, it is my default recommendation.

### Postmark

Renowned for fast, reliable transactional deliverability and great analytics.

Postmark built its reputation on one principle: it only wanted transactional mail, and it policed its network aggressively to keep sending reputation pristine. The result is some of the best inbox placement and lowest latency you can buy. The dashboard's message streams, detailed event logs, and inbound parsing are excellent, and their support is unusually responsive. The trade-offs are that it is deliberately strict about bulk and marketing content, and the per-email price is higher than SES. If a delayed password reset or a lost receipt is a serious problem for your business, Postmark is the safest choice on this list.

### SendGrid

Battle-tested at scale with a huge feature set.

Now part of Twilio, SendGrid is the enterprise workhorse. It handles enormous volume, offers both transactional and marketing products, and has libraries for every language. If you need dedicated IPs, subusers for a multi-tenant platform, and detailed compliance controls, it covers all of it. The downside is that the breadth shows its age: the dashboard is busier, the API surface is larger and less elegant, and newcomers often find the setup fiddly compared to Resend or Postmark. Choose it when you need one mature platform that does everything and will not be out-scaled.

### Amazon SES

The cheapest at volume if you're comfortable wiring it up yourself.

SES is raw infrastructure, priced accordingly: fractions of a cent per email, effectively unbeatable at millions of messages a month. What you give up is the polish. There is no nice template builder, no out-of-the-box analytics dashboard, and you will assemble your own layer for bounce handling (via SNS/SQS), suppression lists, and event tracking. You also start in a sending sandbox and must request production access and higher limits. For teams already deep in AWS with the ops capacity to own the plumbing, SES is the economical backbone. For everyone else, the engineering time erases the savings.

### Loops / Mailgun

Strong options for product and marketing email respectively.

Loops is a newer, design-forward platform aimed at product and lifecycle email (onboarding sequences, drip campaigns, and announcements) with a pleasant visual editor and a developer API behind it. Mailgun, by contrast, is a long-standing developer favourite with strong EU-region support, flexible routing, and powerful inbound email parsing. Reach for Loops when marketing and lifecycle messaging is the job; reach for Mailgun when you want programmable routing and European data residency.

## Common mistakes to avoid

The most frequent failure is skipping proper domain authentication. If SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are not all passing, your mail is a coin flip to reach the inbox no matter which provider you pay. The second is mixing transactional and bulk mail on the same sending domain, where one bad campaign can tank the reputation that carries your password resets. Use a subdomain (like `mail.yourapp.com`) and, ideally, separate domains for marketing and transactional streams. Third, teams forget to handle webhooks for bounces and complaints, so they keep mailing dead addresses and quietly destroy their sender score. Finally, do not hard-code a single provider deep into your codebase; wrap sending behind a small interface so switching later is a one-file change.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which should I choose if I'm just starting out?**
Start with the option that has the gentlest learning curve and best documentation, then switch once you understand your needs better. For most new projects that means Resend: you will be sending authenticated mail within the hour.

**Is the most popular option always the best?**
No. Popularity means more tutorials and community help, but the *right* choice depends on your specific stack, budget, and goals. SendGrid is the most widely deployed, but plenty of teams are happier on Postmark or Resend.

**Do I really need a dedicated IP?**
Not until you are sending consistently high volume (think hundreds of thousands a month). Below that, a shared IP pool managed by the provider usually delivers better, because a warmed shared pool has more established reputation than a cold dedicated IP you have to nurse yourself.

**How do I keep OTP and reset emails out of spam?**
Authenticate your domain fully, keep transactional mail on its own subdomain, write plain subject lines, and monitor bounce/complaint webhooks so you stop mailing dead addresses. Latency matters too, since a provider like Postmark that delivers in seconds beats one that queues for minutes when a user is staring at a login screen.

**Can I migrate later without downtime?**
Yes, if you abstracted the send call behind an interface. Warm up the new domain gradually, run both providers in parallel for a week, then cut over once delivery metrics on the new provider match the old.

## Final verdict

Resend offers the best developer experience for most apps; choose Postmark when deliverability is mission-critical, or SES to minimise cost at scale. If you are building a modern JavaScript product and want to stop thinking about email, start with Resend today, keep your authentication records clean, and revisit the heavier platforms only when your volume or compliance needs genuinely outgrow it.
