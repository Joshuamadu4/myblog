## Introduction

Accepting payments in Nigeria means picking a gateway that handles cards, bank transfers, and USSD reliably, with sane fees and a developer-friendly API. Here are the leaders.

The Nigerian payments landscape has its own rules, and they matter more than any tutorial from Silicon Valley will tell you. Card penetration is lower than in the West, so bank transfers and USSD are not fallbacks; they are primary rails that many customers prefer. Settlement happens in naira, often next-day, and the Central Bank's regulations shape what each provider can legally do. On top of that, "it works on test" and "it works at 9pm on payday when half of Lagos is checking out at once" are very different bars. The gateway you choose becomes load-bearing infrastructure, so choose it with the same care you would a database.

Here are the gateways worth considering, what sets each apart, and where I would start. Everything below assumes you are collecting payments from Nigerian customers; billing international customers is a separate problem addressed at the end.

## What to look for

Before comparing names, get clear on your own priorities. The factors that matter most here:

- **Fit for your stack.** The best tool is the one that matches how you already work. Check for an official SDK in your language, a drop-in checkout widget, and clear webhook documentation.
- **Pricing and value.** Weigh the real cost against the time it saves you. Compare the local-card percentage, the per-transaction cap, transfer fees, and international-card rates, because the headline number hides the details.
- **Developer experience.** Good docs and a clean API compound every single day. Test-mode keys, a transaction dashboard, and reliable webhooks are non-negotiable.
- **Scalability.** Will it still fit when your project (or team) grows? Consider payout APIs, split payments for marketplaces, subscriptions, and virtual accounts.

The factor that trips up newcomers most is **verifying payments correctly**. Never mark an order as paid based on the browser redirect alone, because a user can close the tab or a network can drop. Always verify server-side, either by calling the provider's verify endpoint with the transaction reference or by trusting a signed webhook. Every provider below supports both; using them properly is what separates a reliable integration from a source of silent revenue loss and double-charges.

## How we compared

Each gateway was judged on local payment-method coverage (cards, transfers, USSD, QR), transparency and competitiveness of fees, the quality of the API and documentation, webhook reliability, and the maturity of advanced features like split payments, subscriptions, and virtual accounts. Reliability during peak load and the smoothness of KYC onboarding also weighed in, since both directly affect whether you can actually launch.

## The top picks

### Paystack

Clean API, excellent docs, and reliable local payments. The developer favourite in Nigeria.

Paystack (now part of Stripe) set the standard for developer experience in the Nigerian market and still leads it. The documentation is clear, the dashboard is clean, test mode is painless, and the drop-in Popup and Inline checkout get you collecting payments in an afternoon. It handles cards, bank transfers, USSD, and QR, and the APIs for subscriptions, payment pages, and transfers are well designed. Webhooks are reliable and signed, which makes server-side verification straightforward. The Stripe acquisition brings long-term stability and resources. For the majority of Nigerian developers building a product from scratch, Paystack is the default and the easiest to recommend.

### Flutterwave

Broad pan-African coverage and many payment methods, good for cross-border.

Flutterwave's superpower is reach. If you need to collect payments across Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, and beyond, in multiple currencies and via mobile money as well as cards, Flutterwave covers more ground than anyone. It supports a wide menu of payment methods and offers tools like payment links and virtual cards. The trade-off is that the broad surface area can make the developer experience feel less tidy than Paystack's, and some teams report more variability in support. Choose Flutterwave when pan-African or multi-currency collection is a core requirement rather than a nice-to-have.

### Monnify

Competitive fees and strong bank-transfer/virtual-account support.

Monnify (by TeamApt/Moniepoint) has carved out a strong position on price and on bank-transfer-first flows. Its reserved (dedicated) virtual account feature is excellent: you assign each customer a permanent account number, they transfer money to it, and you get an instant webhook, perfect for wallets, savings apps, and any product where customers prefer transfers to cards. Fees are competitive, often undercutting rivals on transfers. If your business model leans on account-to-account payments rather than cards, Monnify deserves a serious look.

### Squad (by GTCO)

Growing option with virtual accounts and competitive pricing.

Squad, backed by Guaranty Trust Holding Company, is the newest serious contender. Being bank-backed gives it credibility and solid settlement, and it offers virtual accounts, card collection, and competitive pricing as it fights for market share. The ecosystem and community are smaller than Paystack's, so you will find fewer third-party tutorials, but the core product is capable. It is worth evaluating especially if you already bank with GTCO or want to diversify away from a single provider.

### Stripe (via workarounds)

Not officially local, but relevant for developers billing internationally.

Stripe does not support Nigerian businesses collecting local payments directly. It appears on this list because many Nigerian developers build for a global audience and need to bill customers abroad. Teams typically achieve this by incorporating a company in a supported jurisdiction (often via Stripe Atlas or a US/UK LLC) and running Stripe through that entity. It is the gold standard for international card payments and subscriptions, but it is not a solution for collecting naira from local customers, so use Paystack or Flutterwave for that.

## Common mistakes to avoid

The number-one mistake is trusting the client-side redirect instead of verifying the transaction server-side. Always confirm with the verify endpoint or a signed webhook before releasing goods. Second, failing to validate webhook signatures leaves you open to spoofed "payment successful" calls; every provider gives you a secret to check, so check it. Third, teams hard-code a single provider throughout the codebase and then suffer when that provider has an outage on a busy day, so wrap payment logic behind an interface so you can add a fallback. Fourth, handle idempotency: retried webhooks and double-clicked checkout buttons will generate duplicate events, so key your order updates on the transaction reference. Finally, do not skip KYC early, because business verification can take days, and discovering that on launch day is avoidable pain.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which should I choose if I'm just starting out?**
Start with the option that has the gentlest learning curve and best documentation, then switch once you understand your needs better. For most Nigerian startups that is Paystack; you will be collecting live payments the same week.

**Is the most popular option always the best?**
No. Popularity means more tutorials and community help, but the *right* choice depends on your specific stack, budget, and goals. A transfer-heavy wallet app may be better served by Monnify's virtual accounts than by the most popular card gateway.

**Cards, transfers, or USSD: which should I support?**
Support as many as your provider makes easy, because Nigerian customers split across all three. At minimum enable cards and bank transfers; add USSD if you serve customers who prefer not to use cards online. Dedicated virtual accounts are the smoothest transfer experience if your model fits them.

**How do I collect payments from international customers?**
Use Paystack or Flutterwave for naira, and a separate Stripe or PayPal setup (usually via a foreign-incorporated entity) for foreign-currency billing. Keep the two flows cleanly separated in your code and your accounting.

**What about subscriptions and recurring billing?**
Paystack and Flutterwave both support recurring charges and plans. Remember that recurring card charges depend on the card supporting it and on CBN rules around card tokenisation, so build a graceful fallback (an email with a payment link) for renewals that fail.

## Final verdict

For most Nigerian developers Paystack offers the best API and docs; choose Flutterwave when you need wider pan-African coverage. If transfers dominate your model, weigh Monnify seriously, and reach for Stripe only when you are billing customers abroad. Whatever you pick, verify every payment on the server, validate your webhooks, and keep the integration behind an interface so adding a second provider later is a small change rather than a rewrite.
